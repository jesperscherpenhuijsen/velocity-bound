# Cover letter: A velocity bound for positive-energy warp shells

Oct 20, 2026 · @Jesper Scherpenhuijsen

To the Editors, Classical and Quantum Gravity

Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, *A velocity bound for positive-energy warp shells*, for publication in Classical and Quantum Gravity as a regular paper.

Fuchs et al. (Class. Quantum Grav. 41, 095013, 2024) constructed a stationary metric from a static matter shell with a shift vector in its wall that satisfies the null, weak, strong and dominant energy conditions at a shift amplitude of 0.02c. They noted that a larger shift must eventually violate the conditions through the momentum flux it induces, and left the value of that limit to future work. The manuscript determines it.

The paper contains three results. First, a theorem: for any static shell and radial shift profile, the Eulerian energy density and stresses change only at second order in the shift amplitude while the momentum density is linear, so the null condition fails at an exact first-order threshold v\* = min (ρ⁰ + S⁰(e,e))/(2J·e). Second, a closed form for the linearized momentum density J on a static spherically symmetric background, and a bound obtained from it: since a unit transition of width Δ has second derivative at least 4/Δ², the threshold obeys v\* ≤ 2πG(ρ + p\_t)Δ²/c⁴, which for a uniform shell is about (3/4)C(1 − R₁/R₂) with C the compactness. Third, an independent numerical check: the published shell reaches 0.031c by bisection on the full energy conditions, and eight shells follow the predicted scaling, with the one-dimensional closed form reproducing the three-dimensional thresholds to three digits.

The result complements recent work on the same class. Le (arXiv:2602.18023, 2605.25417, 2606.22531) certified the published shell with an all-observer method, located energy-condition failures of source-first shells at the source–vacuum transition, and derived an acceleration–compactness ceiling for radiating thin shells; the present bound is its constant-velocity counterpart. The manuscript also takes account of the critique of Barzegar, Buchert and Vigneron (arXiv:2602.16495): it bounds the shift amplitude of the metric as written and makes no claim about its interpretation as a transport velocity or about a realizing matter model.
